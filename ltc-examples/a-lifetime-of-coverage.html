Four Months of Care. A Lifetime of Coverage.

Four guaranteed long-term care designs for a 50-year-old couple — built for partners and executives who retire early, live long, and expect private-room care.

Illustrative examples only. Figures are taken from carrier illustrations for a healthy California couple, both age 50, Preferred Non-Tobacco. Actual premiums and benefits vary by age, health, underwriting, and policy design.

Partners at the Big 4 typically retire near 60. That means retirement may last thirty years or longer — and inside that window, one extended care event can quietly become the largest uninsured financial risk a family will ever face.

The four illustrations below demonstrate a planning strategy built for professionals who value guarantees, tax efficiency, and capital preservation. Each one is funded once. Each one pays lifetime long-term care benefits. Each one returns more to the family than the premium if care is never needed. And each one develops guaranteed cash value immediately, so the capital genuinely at risk is a fraction of the premium paid.

Every illustration shown here is fully customizable. Monthly benefits, inflation protection, payment schedules, and death benefits can all be tailored to your family’s objectives.

What Every Illustration Provides

  • Lifetime long-term care benefits — no cap, no time limit
  • A guaranteed death benefit greater than the premium if care is never needed
  • Immediate guaranteed cash value, available by policy loan
  • A qualified long-term care premium component that may carry a tax advantage
  • One-time funding — no future premiums, no future increases

Why This Strategy Is Different

Traditional long-term care insurance asks one question: “How much coverage do you want?” This design asks a better one.

“How little capital must I actually place at risk to secure lifetime protection?”

That difference changes everything. It moves the decision from a coverage question to a capital question — which is exactly how a partner evaluates every other allocation on the balance sheet.

Five Reasons Partners and Executives Prefer This Design

  1. Lifetime Benefits

    Most long-term care policies stop paying after two to five years. These designs provide guaranteed lifetime benefits for eligible claims. If care lasts ten years, or twenty, or longer, the benefits continue.

  2. If Care Is Never Needed, the Family Still Comes Out Ahead

    Unlike traditional long-term care insurance, the premium is not simply consumed. Every illustration guarantees a death benefit larger than the amount committed.

    Illustration 2 — $20,000 per month per insured

    Single premium  $271,867   ·   Guaranteed death benefit  $480,000
    Guaranteed gain to beneficiaries  $208,133

    No claim. No lapse. No outcome in which the premium simply disappears.

  3. Your Capital Is Still Working

    Immediately after issue, every policy develops guaranteed cash surrender value that may be accessed by policy loan. Accordingly, the capital genuinely at risk is substantially less than the premium paid.

    Illustration 2 — the capital actually at risk

    Single premium  $271,867   less   Year-1 guaranteed cash value  $114,605
    Net capital at risk  $157,262

    That is fewer than four months of the benefit this policy pays. Beyond that point, the insurance company continues paying eligible benefits for life.

  4. A Meaningful Tax Advantage

    Every illustration identifies the portion of the premium attributable to qualified long-term care coverage. Depending on how the policy is owned — individually, or through a partnership, S corporation, or C corporation — that component may support a deduction and reduce the effective cost of the protection. The rules differ by structure and are subject to annual limits, so the planning is worth doing before the policy is issued rather than after.

  5. A Patented Design

    This is not simply another long-term care policy. It is a patented policy design that combines lifetime long-term care benefits, immediate guaranteed cash value, a guaranteed death benefit, return-of-capital characteristics, and potential tax advantages into a single integrated solution — available through a limited number of carriers.

A Planning Opportunity for Partners and Business Owners

Where a policy is owned by a partnership, an S corporation, or a C corporation, the qualified long-term care premium component may be treated differently than it would be for an individual purchaser. The structure that produces the best result is not the same for every household, and it is far easier to establish before the policy is issued than to correct afterward. This is precisely the analysis a CPA should be doing alongside the insurance decision — and it is included in every review conducted here at no cost.

Consult your CPA or tax adviser regarding your individual circumstances.
