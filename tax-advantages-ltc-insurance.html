California · 2026 Tax Planning

Federal & California Tax Advantages
of Long-Term Care Insurance

Long-term care insurance may offer valuable tax benefits.

Long-term care insurance can provide more than protection against the cost of care. Depending on how your policy is owned and funded, it may also provide significant federal tax advantages.

Federal Tax Highlights

Individuals

Premiums for federally tax-qualified policies may qualify as deductible medical expenses, subject to IRS age-based limits and applicable tax rules.

Self-Employed Individuals

Sole proprietors, partners, and certain S-Corporation owners may qualify for an above-the-line federal deduction for eligible premiums, subject to IRS limitations.

C-Corporations

Potentially the Most Efficient

A C-Corporation may generally deduct the qualifying long-term care insurance premiums it pays for eligible employees, including employee-owners, without being limited by the individual age-based deduction limits. This can make a C-Corporation one of the most tax-efficient ways to fund long-term care protection.

Asset-Based Long-Term Care

Many modern policies combine long-term care benefits with life insurance. Qualified long-term care benefits are generally received income-tax-free, and if care is never needed, a generally income-tax-free death benefit may pass to beneficiaries.

Federal deduction limits are adjusted annually by the IRS. During your complimentary review, I will explain the current limits that apply to your situation.

California Tax Considerations

California generally follows many federal tax rules for qualified long-term care benefits but does not always conform to every federal deduction available, particularly for certain self-employed deductions. California tax treatment depends on your individual circumstances.

Because California tax law differs from federal law in several areas, professional guidance is essential before making funding decisions.

Every Situation Is Different

The most tax-efficient funding strategy depends upon factors such as:

  • Individual or business ownership
  • Self-employed status
  • C-Corporation ownership
  • Existing assets
  • Health Savings Accounts (HSAs)
  • Asset-based versus traditional long-term care insurance

As both a CPA and a Licensed Insurance Broker, I help clients evaluate these alternatives and determine the most appropriate strategy for their circumstances.
