Rather than beginning with a new proposal, I begin with the policies you already own. That independent approach frequently identifies opportunities to strengthen guarantees, enhance living benefits, reduce unnecessary long-term costs, improve capital efficiency, and preserve family wealth.

“Is your existing coverage already the best solution?” Sometimes the answer is yes. Sometimes it is surprisingly not.

Your Insurance Deserves the Same Oversight as Your Investments

Life and long-term care policies are frequently purchased, filed away, and forgotten. Years pass, families change, and the policy quietly stops matching the plan it was bought to support. An older policy may contain:

  • Guarantees that are weaker than the owner expects
  • Premiums that may rise substantially in later years
  • Benefits that no longer fit the family’s needs
  • Conversion privileges that are approaching expiration
  • Living benefits the policyholder did not know existed
  • Opportunities to increase protection or improve capital efficiency

None of these are visible from a premium notice. They are visible from the policy itself — and from the illustrations behind it.
